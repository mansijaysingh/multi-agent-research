# use of drones for pollination in apple orchards

## Introduction

The agricultural industry is increasingly exploring innovative technologies to address traditional farming challenges. Recently, drones have been introduced for artificial pollination in apple orchards [3]. This technique involves using a hexacopter drone to spread pollen across apple trees [1][2]. Prior to apple orchards, this method had already been utilized for cherry and almond crops [1][2].

## Key Findings

Drone pollination offers several notable advantages in terms of speed, efficiency, and cost:
* **Speed and Coverage:** Drones can cover and pollinate a significantly larger area at a much faster rate than bees [1][2]. Specifically, a drone can accomplish in just one hour what it would take bees two weeks to do [1][2].
* **Environmental and Health Benefits:** Drones are being used for artificial pollination in apple orchards due to the disappearance of honeybees caused by climate change [3]. The drone flies over the trees, spraying a solution mixed with pollens and honey [3]. Furthermore, drone pollination prevents the spread of fire blight by blocking outside germs from entering orchards, and the wind generated by the drone increases pollination efficiency [3].
* **Economic Costs:** Using a drone is about 25 percent less expensive than employing human workers for pollination [3].

Currently, researchers are investigating whether this pollination method results in higher and better quality yields [2]. Additionally, the city of Sasan plans to test this project at 14 apple orchards to check the condition of the pollinated fruit before expanding the use of drones [3].

## Conclusion

The use of drones for pollination in apple orchards represents a promising advancement in agricultural technology. With benefits such as reduced labor costs, increased speed compared to natural bee pollination, and protection against certain diseases like fire blight, drone technology offers a viable alternative to address challenges like the decline of honeybee populations. Ongoing research and upcoming tests, such as those planned in Sasan, will continue to evaluate the impact of drone pollination on fruit condition and overall yield quality.
