# biochar adoption by smallholder farmers in Kenya

## Introduction

In Kenya, smallholder farmers cultivating less than five acres form the primary target market for biochar adoption [1]. Despite being resource-constrained and having limited capital, these farmers are motivated to adopt biochar because it restores soil fertility, improves crop yields, and provides measurable returns within a single growing season [1]. Cooperative structures assist these farmers in overcoming individual market barriers [1]. However, declining soil fertility and low uptake of sustainable agricultural technologies like biochar continue to hinder productivity among Kenyan smallholder farmers [3]. Although biochar is considered a cost-effective soil amendment, socio-economic barriers prevent its widespread adoption [3]. To better understand household production and use, a transdisciplinary research project in Kenya studied 150 farming households across three locations that produced biochar using locally manufactured GASTOV Top-lit Updraft (TLUD) gasifier cookstoves and applied it to their own fields, building on long-term field trials established in 2006 [2]. Additionally, researchers measured fuel use, biochar production potential, and indoor concentrations of carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, and fine particulate matter in 75 of these households [2].

## Key Findings

A study conducted in the sub-humid regions of Western Kenya utilized a multistage stratified sampling procedure and a Heckman selection model to investigate how socio-economic characteristics affect the adoption intensity of biochar [3]. The research identified that biochar awareness, education levels, credit access, and total land used in farming positively influence adoption [3]. Conversely, total land owned negatively impacts biochar adoption [3]. Driven by smallholder adoption, Kenya's biochar market is projected to grow from US$10.99 million in 2026 to US$30.94 million by 2034 [1].

## Conclusion

Successful market development for biochar in Kenya depends on understanding farmer economics and providing comprehensive support, which includes financing solutions, technical assistance, and quality products [1].
