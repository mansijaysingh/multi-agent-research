# zinc-air battery recycling in India

## Introduction

Zinc-based battery technology is gaining attention in India due to zinc's natural abundance, low cost, high recyclability, and easy replaceability [1]. Researchers have developed a zinc-air battery that could transform India's energy landscape, offering a practical and cost-effective alternative to lithium-ion batteries while significantly reducing the need to import them [2]. Globally, approximately 80 percent of zinc comes from recycled materials through a simple, low-energy recycling process [1]. Furthermore, used zinc battery cassette packs can be individually removed and swapped with fully charged ones, unlike lithium-ion systems that require replacing entire battery packs [1]. Zinc-air batteries have been researched for about a century, already function well in low-discharge devices like sonobuoys and hearing aids, and have recently seen advancements such as water-based electrolytes and wider operating temperatures, with artificial intelligence expected to help accelerate manufacturing breakthroughs [1].

## Key Findings

End-of-life zinc-air hearing aid batteries are largely ignored by current recycling frameworks due to a combination of factors, including the non-existence of recycling rules and targets, their non-hazardous stature which sets them apart from other battery types, and the common practice of abandonment [3]. Despite this lack of current recycling efforts, implementing comprehensive zinc battery recycling strategies is essential for supporting a circular economy and maximizing resource recovery [4]. This process involves specific methods to safely handle and process spent batteries in order to retrieve valuable materials [4]. By adopting these strategies, industries can conserve natural resources and significantly reduce environmental waste [4]. Proper recycling infrastructure ensures that recovered zinc can be efficiently reintroduced into the manufacturing supply chain [4]. Ultimately, these integrated approaches promote sustainability and reduce the overall environmental impact of battery disposal and production [4].

## Conclusion

Zinc-air battery technology presents a promising, cost-effective, and domestically available energy solution for India that reduces reliance on imported lithium-ion systems [1][2]. Although end-of-life zinc-air batteries are currently overlooked by existing recycling frameworks and often abandoned due to their non-hazardous nature and a lack of specific rules and targets [3], establishing comprehensive recycling strategies is crucial [4]. Implementing proper recycling infrastructure and safety methods allows valuable materials to be retrieved and efficiently reintroduced into the manufacturing supply chain [4]. Ultimately, these integrated approaches support a circular economy, conserve natural resources, promote sustainability, and reduce the overall environmental impact of battery production and disposal [4].
