# zinc-air battery recycling in India

## Introduction

Zinc-based technology is gaining market enthusiasm in India due to the easy replaceability and recyclability of zinc [1]. Zinc is an abundant, low-cost, environmentally friendly, and recyclable metal that has been researched for about a century [1]. The zinc battery recycling technology landscape is currently in an emerging growth phase, driven by environmental regulations and circular economy initiatives [5]. Furthermore, zinc-based technologies are critical in the global energy storage landscape due to zinc's abundance, cost-effectiveness, and safety characteristics [5]. Modern zinc battery systems range from traditional cells to zinc-air and zinc-ion systems, each presenting unique recycling challenges and opportunities [5]. Current market dynamics indicate exponential growth in deployment across grid-scale energy storage, electric vehicles, and consumer electronics, which requires proactive development of recycling capabilities to prevent waste management crises and ensure sustainable material supply chains [5].

## Key Findings

- Globally, about 80% of zinc comes from recycled materials, and the battery recycling process is straightforward and less energy-intensive [1].
- Used zinc battery cassette packs can be easily removed and replaced with fully charged ones, unlike entire lithium-ion battery packs [1].
- Researchers at the Indian Institute of Technology Madras (IIT) are developing rechargeable zinc-air batteries as an alternative to lithium-ion batteries for electric vehicles in India, proposing separate Zinc Recharge Stations where motorists can swap used zinc cassettes for fully charged ones that could potentially be recharged using solar panels [2].
- Alkaline zinc-air and zinc-carbon batteries can be recycled up to 99.9% using advanced mechanical and chemical processes [4].
- The recycling process sorts materials into three main end products: steel, paper and plastic, and zinc and manganese concentrate, which are taken back to facilities to be reused in the creation of new products [4].
- Battery recycling in India operates on an Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) certificate system [4].
- The general recycling procedure involves collection, crushing, sorting, sieving, and utilizing hydrometallurgical and pyrometallurgical processes [4].

## Conclusion

Zinc-air battery recycling in India benefits from the straightforward, less energy-intensive nature of recycling zinc, a metal where about 80% globally comes from recycled materials [1]. Advanced mechanical and chemical processes—including collection, crushing, sorting, sieving, and hydrometallurgical and pyrometallurgical methods—allow alkaline zinc-air and zinc-carbon batteries to be recycled up to 99.9% into steel, paper and plastic, and zinc and manganese concentrate for reuse [4]. In India, this battery recycling operates on an Extended Producer Responsibility certificate system [4]. With innovations like swappable zinc cassettes being developed for electric vehicles, proactive development of these recycling capabilities remains essential to support sustainable material supply chains and prevent waste management crises as deployment expands [2][5].
