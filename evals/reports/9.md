# current trends in quantum computing hardware

## Introduction

Quantum computing has evolved from a narrow niche discipline into a tool that could play a prominent role in solving complex problems [4]. The hardware technology required for quantum computing remains in flux, with superconducting qubits and trapped ions being the most widely known approaches alongside other upcoming methods like neutral atoms, photonics, and silicon qubits [4]. Rapid advancements in hardware designs and manufacturing techniques over the last ten years have aimed to create a commercially relevant quantum computer [2]. The global quantum computing hardware market was valued at $702 million in 2025 and is projected to reach $15.5 billion by 2035 [5], with another report projecting the market to surpass US$21 billion by 2046 [2]. North America holds the largest share of the hardware market in 2025, while the Asia Pacific region is projected to experience the fastest growth during the forecast period [5].

## Key Findings

A key hardware trend for 2025 is an increase in experiments involving logical qubits, showing a shift from discussing physical qubits to demonstrating logical qubits and proving they reduce error rates [1]. This growth requires accelerated experimentation, as logical qubit-based systems are promised within the next few years [1]. Additional hardware trends involve more specialized hardware instead of universal quantum computing, networking noisy intermediate-scale quantum devices together, and improved and novel physical qubits, alongside more software abstraction layers and workforce development tools [1]. The hardware market spans eight technology categories, which include superconducting, photonic, trapped-ion, and neutral atom systems [2]. Recent breakthroughs are driving technology closer to solving critical problems such as drug discovery, battery chemistry, materials simulations, and fraud detection [2]. Furthermore, defense and national-security applications account for $131.8 million of the 2025 market and are expected to reach $2,798 million by 2035, driven by post-quantum security policies and research in quantum sensing, communications, and cryptanalysis [5].

## Conclusion

The quantum computing industry is reaching an inflection point where it must demonstrate scalability, realize practical use-cases, and secure early adopters within the next few years [2]. To capitalize on these opportunities, businesses are recommended to invest in research and development and form strategic partnerships, while also ensuring compliance with evolving regulations [3]. Ultimately, ongoing research aims to create widely usable solutions, as the necessary systems and software for quantum computing are currently limited [4].
