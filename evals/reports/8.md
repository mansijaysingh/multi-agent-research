# recent progress in solid-state batteries

## Introduction

Recent advances in solid-state batteries have made progress in ion transport, interfacial stability, and scalable manufacturing [1]. Solid electrolytes play a crucial role in performance because they require mechanical strength, ionic conductivity, thermal stability, and chemical stability [1]. The field is moving past academic research toward commercial readiness, which is indicated by an increase in patent filings [3]. This growth is driven by the demand for renewable energy grid storage, electric vehicles, and safer, high-performance energy solutions [3]. 

## Key Findings

Recent prototypes show significant advancements, including Sunwoda's 400 Wh/kg polymer battery and Chery's 600 Wh/kg module [3]. These innovations could offer electric vehicles a cycle life of 1,200 cycles and ranges exceeding 1,000 kilometers [3]. Based on an annual driving distance of 20,000 kilometers, this performance potentially enables over a decade of use [3]. Additionally, a recent study introduced a structural engineering strategy to improve battery performance and safety without expensive metal additives [1]. This approach modified zirconium-based halide solid electrolytes by adding divalent anions such as sulfur and oxygen, which lowered diffusion barriers and widened lithium-ion migration pathways within the crystal structure [1].

Regarding commercialization and company timelines, Samsung SDI aims to mass-produce solid-state batteries by 2027, and its initial samples have received positive customer feedback [2]. These batteries use a silver-carbon composite anode to improve stability and prevent dendrite formation, targeting premium applications [2]. Prototypes from Samsung demonstrate a driving range between 800 and 1,000 kilometers, a lifespan of over 1,000 cycles, and 40% higher energy density than current prismatic cells [2]. 

Toyota is developing solid-state batteries for electric vehicles with a target production window between 2027 and 2028, aiming for a 750-mile range with a 10-minute charge, better safety, higher energy density, and shorter charging times [2]. However, Toyota updated its strategy in June 2023 to state it will not use commercial solid-state batteries until at least 2027 [4]. Other manufacturing plans include Honda's 2022 intention to operate a demonstration line for all-solid-state batteries in early 2024, Nissan's plan to launch an electric vehicle with in-house developed all-solid-state batteries by fiscal year 2028, and a September 2024 partnership between ProLogium and OCSiAl to strengthen European high-performance battery supply chains [4]. 

Despite these developments, real commercialization is still years away [5]. QuantumScape pushed back its initial commercial production goal of 2024, shifting instead to shipping samples to launch customers in 2025 and beginning field testing in 2026 [5]. IDTechEx predicts that larger production-volume vehicles will not see solid-state batteries until the 2030s [5]. Solid-state battery capacity is projected to reach just over 100 GWh by 2035, compared to approximately 3,800 GWh for the overall electric vehicle market [5].

## Conclusion

Progress is being made on solid-state batteries, and observers remain cautiously optimistic with healthy skepticism, noting that meaningful progress toward safer and better batteries is occurring despite missing the revolutionary timelines promised a decade ago [5].
