# recent progress in solid-state batteries

## Introduction

Recent progress in solid-state batteries has advanced scalable manufacturing, ion transport, and interfacial stability [1]. Solid electrolytes serve a central role in this performance, which requires a combination of thermal, chemical, redox, and mechanical stability, along with ionic conductivity [1]. Ongoing developments in pilot production lines, research, and gigafactories are helping to enhance the performance and reduce the costs of solid-state batteries [4]. Driven by the electrification of transportation and the need for sustainable energy storage, there is an urgent demand for safer, high-performance energy solutions that operate in harsher environments [3][4]. These factors ultimately position solid-state batteries as a key technology for transportation and sustainable energy storage [4].

## Key Findings

Recent prototypes demonstrate significant momentum in solid-state battery technology [3]. For example, Chery's module has achieved an energy density of 600 Wh/kg, while Sunwoda's polymer battery has reached 400 Wh/kg with 1,200 cycles [3]. These types of advancements could potentially provide electric vehicles with over a decade of use and ranges exceeding 1,000 kilometers [3].

In material and structural advancements, a recent study introduced a structural engineering strategy to improve battery safety and performance without expensive metal additives [1]. This approach modifies zirconium-based halide solid electrolytes through the addition of divalent anions such as sulfur and oxygen [1]. Consequently, the crystal structure develops lower diffusion barriers and wider lithium-ion migration pathways [1]. Additionally, advancements in cell design and materials science continue to make solid-state batteries increasingly viable as a next-generation technology, with pressure effects and temperature performance emerging as recent focus areas in development [4].

Major companies are actively advancing toward commercialization with ambitious production targets. Samsung SDI is progressing with a target for mass production by 2027 and has received positive feedback on initial samples [2]. Samsung's solid-state cells use a silver-carbon composite layer as the anode to enhance stability and prevent dendrite formation, achieving a lifespan of over 1,000 cycles and aiming for ranges of 800 to 1,000 kilometers with an energy density 40% higher than current prismatic cells [2]. Simultaneously, Toyota is accelerating its development for electric vehicles with an aim for production between 2027 and 2028, targeting a 750-mile range, a ten-minute charging time, higher energy density, and improved safety compared to traditional lithium-ion technology [2].

Beyond individual company milestones, a sharp growth in patents indicates that the field is moving past academic research and toward commercial readiness [3]. This surge in intellectual property is driven by the demand for high-performance, safer energy solutions [3]. Furthermore, patent activity highlights shifting global dynamics, showing steady leadership from Japan alongside a rapid rise in China driven by both new entrants and established firms [3].

## Conclusion

Recent progress in solid-state batteries highlights their growing viability as a next-generation technology for sustainable energy storage and transportation [4]. Through breakthroughs in material science, structural engineering, and prototype performance—alongside substantial investments and production targets set by major industry players for the late 2020s—the field is successfully transitioning from academic research toward commercial readiness [1][2][3][4].
