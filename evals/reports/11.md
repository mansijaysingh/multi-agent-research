# use of drones for pollination in apple orchards

## Introduction

Apple orchard management practices are seeing innovative changes through the integration of new technology [1]. Specifically, the Beak and Skiff Apple Orchard in LaFayette made history by becoming the first apple orchard to use drones for tree pollination [1]. Partnering with the start-up company Dropcopter, this method brought a unique approach to traditional orchard management [1][2]. 

## Key Findings

Dropcopter utilized a hexacopter drone to spray pollen directly onto the apple trees, covering a five-acre area of the orchard [1][2]. During the application, the drone hovers approximately eight feet above the apple trees to deliver the necessary pollen [3]. According to co-founder Adam Fine, this achievement marked a world first in pollination [2]. 

This drone-based method provides greater precision than traditional pollination performed by bees [3]. Furthermore, drone-assisted pollination combines modern navigation with artificial intelligence, which helps unmanned aerial vehicles evaluate flower conditions, determine optimal pollination timing, and adapt to real-time environmental and weather data [4]. These multi-purpose drones can also target specific areas to apply pesticides and fertilizers precisely, which reduces worker exposure and chemical waste [4]. While traditional manual pollination requires between 7 and 14 laborers to service 1,000 trees over a three-day period, a single operator using a drone can pollinate one tree every minute [4]. However, drone operations do require pausing every 16 trees for a five-minute period to refill pollen and change the battery [4].

## Conclusion

The historic use of drones for pollination at the Beak and Skiff Apple Orchard demonstrates a significant departure from traditional orchard management practices [1]. By leveraging technologies such as hexacopters and artificial intelligence, orchard operators can achieve greater precision in pollen delivery compared to bees [1][3][4]. While operations require brief pauses to change batteries and refill pollen, drone technology offers a modernized, efficient alternative to traditional methods [1][4].
