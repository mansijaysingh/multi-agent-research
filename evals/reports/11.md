# use of drones for pollination in apple orchards

## Introduction

The use of drones in agriculture represents a shifting approach to orchard management, particularly as traditional methods face limitations and bee populations decline [4]. Recently, a start-up company named Dropcopter utilized a hexacopter drone to spray pollen over five acres at the Beak & Skiff Apple Orchard in LaFayette, New York, marking the first time a drone was used to pollinate an apple orchard [1][4]. Developed alongside Syracuse, this technology has also been applied to cherry and almond orchards [1][2]. This research report examines the implementation, benefits, and ongoing investigations surrounding the use of drones for pollination in apple orchards.

## Key Findings

* **Efficiency and Coverage:** Drones are capable of covering a large amount of ground in just one hour [1]. Technically, a drone can cover a larger area in an hour than bees can manage in two weeks [2]. 
* **Operational Flexibility:** Drones can operate effectively in cold weather when bees remain dormant [2]. Furthermore, they can assist with pollination by utilizing vibration or airflow, which proves especially useful when bee activity is low [3]. 
* **Yield Impacts:** A 2015 test involving a prototype pollinating drone successfully increased crop yields by ten percent [4]. Researchers are currently investigating whether this pollination method consistently results in higher and better-quality yields [2]. 
* **Capacity and Scale:** Dropcopter currently pollinates up to a maximum of 12 acres while carrying up to one and a half kilos of pollen [1].
* **Broader Orchard Management Benefits:** Operating entirely from the air, drones avoid touching the ground, which eliminates soil compaction and promotes healthy root growth [3]. As a potentially more sustainable alternative to conventional tractor-and-airblast-sprayer methods, electric-powered drones reduce diesel use, lower material usage, decrease soil deterioration from erosion and compaction, shorten the time required for farmers to apply pesticides, reduce labor, save chemicals, and improve overall operational efficiency [3][5]. However, the use of drones for these agricultural tasks also presents certain challenges [5].

## Conclusion

The initial deployment of hexacopter drones for apple orchard pollination demonstrates significant potential for modern agriculture [1][4]. By offering rapid coverage, functioning during cold weather when bees are dormant, and avoiding the soil compaction associated with ground equipment, drones provide a viable operational alternative [2][3]. While they offer sustainability benefits, reduced labor, and increased efficiency, ongoing research is still evaluating their direct impact on yield quality and quantity, alongside the challenges inherent in their use [2][3][5].
