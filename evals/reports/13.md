# the thing about the new rules

## Introduction

This report examines two distinct sets of new regulatory rules: the European Union's GDPR data protection law and proposed or enacted legislation concerning U.S. voting rules, specifically the SAVE America Act and the Make Elections Great Again (MEGA) Act [2][5].

## Key Findings

Regarding data protection, the GDPR requires organizations to determine, document, and notify individuals of the lawful basis for processing data, as well as provide a valid reason if that justification changes [2]. Under these rules, data processing must remain lawful, fair, transparent, and limited to explicitly stated purposes [2]. Furthermore, organizations must collect only the absolute minimum amount of necessary data, keep personal data accurate and up to date, and understand new privacy rights granted to data subjects [2].

In the context of U.S. voting regulations, the Utah Lieutenant Governor's office reported 27 confirmed instances of noncitizen registration and 5,007 registrants with unconfirmed citizenship status on May 27, 2026 [5]. Many of these unconfirmed individuals registered before Social Security or driver license numbers were required, and they were scheduled to receive notices concerning new state citizenship requirements [5]. To address these issues, the SAVE America Act aims to ensure that solely citizens register to vote, serving as an expanded version of earlier legislation introduced in May 2024 [5]. However, critics contend that alternative, easier methods exist to verify citizenship without introducing new barriers [5]. Concurrently, the House is considering the Make Elections Great Again (MEGA) Act, which proposes proof of citizenship and photo ID mandates alongside wider modifications to mail voting and election technology [5].

## Conclusion

The new rules across both domains seek to establish stricter standards and oversight, whether through safeguarding individual data privacy under the GDPR or implementing citizenship verification and identification requirements under voting legislation like the SAVE America Act and the MEGA Act [2][5].
